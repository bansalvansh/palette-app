How the analysis actually works
1. Your photo is passed to MediaPipe Face Landmarker, a pretrained Google model that runs in your browser via WebAssembly and returns 478 facial points if a real face is present. No face found, face too small, or lighting too dark/blown-out → upload rejected rather than guessed at.

2. Before sampling skin color, we run a gray-world white-balance correction across the photo — estimating the ambient light's color cast and correcting for it, so a warm-lit room doesn't fake a warm undertone reading.

3. We sample 10 landmark points across cheeks, forehead, nose bridge and jaw (not eyes, lips, hair, or background), take a trimmed/median value per patch to drop outlier pixels (glare, tiny shadows), then convert to LAB color space. The b channel (blue↔yellow) is the standard signal used to classify warm vs. cool undertone.

4. We also check how far the face is turned from the camera using landmark geometry, and how much the sampled patches agree with each other — both feed into a High/Medium/Low confidence badge shown next to the result, instead of presenting every read as equally certain.

5. Real seasonal color analysis isn't just warm/cool — it also factors in how much contrast there is between your skin, hair and eyes. We sample iris color (from the model's iris landmarks) and a patch above the hairline, then measure how far their lightness sits from your skin tone. High contrast reads toward "Bright/Clear" seasons, low contrast toward "Soft/Muted" ones — both real terms colorists use, not ones we made up. If hair or eyes can't be read cleanly (hat, glare, hair out of frame), this part is skipped rather than guessed.

6. The same photo is passed to MediaPipe Pose Landmarker for body keypoints. We compute the ratio of shoulder width to hip width — the same logic real stylist quizzes use ("are your shoulders wider than your hips?") — using each point's model-reported visibility score to gate whether the read is trustworthy, and checking the shoulder line is roughly horizontal so a tilted or leaning photo doesn't quietly skew the ratio.

7. A single 2D photo genuinely can't measure waist definition reliably (clothing hides it), so the detected body shape shows as a pre-selected, editable option rather than a fixed verdict — you confirm or correct it.

8. Undertone + body shape + your quiz answers are sent to the backend, which scores the catalog and returns matches with live shop links.
